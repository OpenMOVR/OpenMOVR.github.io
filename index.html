How we work

Open by default, careful with what matters

Registry numbers end up in grant applications, publications and policy work. Everything we publish is built to hold up when someone checks it.

OpenMOVR on GitHub Developer docs

Every figure names its population

Published statistics carry their cohort, denominator, data date and app version, so a number quoted elsewhere can be traced back and checked.

The code is public

The app, the analytics library and this site are open source under the MIT license. Methods are reviewable, and anyone can contribute.

Participants are protected

Only aggregates are public, and small cells are suppressed. Record-level data is shared only under a data use agreement.

Where MOVR is going

From registry to open platform

MOVR has run in phases since 2013. Each one built on the last, and the next is shaped by the families who tested it.

  1. 2013 – 2018

    USNDR

    The United States Neuromuscular Disease Registry, MOVR's foundation study, across 26 sites and 4 diseases.

  2. 2019 – 2025

    MOVR Data Hub

    MDA's flagship registry, covering 7 diseases at 60+ sites. Data collection has ended, and the dataset remains open to approved research.

  3. 2025 – 2026

    MOVR 2.0 pilot

    Families living with DMD, SMA and LGMD tested candidate platforms and told us what works. Enrollment is closed.

  4. Next · Q1 2027

    MOVR 2.0 launch

    Recruitment opens for the full MOVR 2.0 platform, shaped by what the pilot taught us.

Part of the Muscular Dystrophy Association

MOVR is MDA's registry. OpenMOVR is its open source space.

MOVR was created by the Muscular Dystrophy Association to capture the real-world experience of people living with neuromuscular disease, across 60+ clinical sites in the United States. OpenMOVR opens that work up: the tools, the methods and the figures.
