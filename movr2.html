The pilot, 2025 – 2026

Highlights from the pilot

The pilot evaluation phase tested how MOVR 2.0 should work, with families living with SMA, DMD and LGMD. A few first principles came out of it. They point to careful, realistic and focused work. These are highlights only. MDA MOVR publishes the official findings and documents.

Trust comes first

Consenting online was easy. Trust in MDA is a big part of why people took part, and it has to be kept at every step after.

Start from the questions

Each dataset begins with a question from the community and a short list of the data that answers it, never the most data possible.

Measure the gap, then close it

Pull the full record, check it against that list, and report honestly what is missing. Then fill the gap from the right source, whether a document, a note or a survey.

Build to extend

No single source is enough. Harmonize from day one so surveys, wearables and biospecimens can be added later without starting over.

Health records

What data standards can do, and what still needs building

MOVR 2.0 starts from the records participants authorize. HL7 FHIR, the standard US health record systems are required to support, carries the structured part of a record well. Much of what matters in neuromuscular disease lives outside it.

What HL7 FHIR carries well

Diagnoses, medications and lab results, already coded to SNOMED CT, RxNorm and LOINC. Under the 21st Century Cures Act, certified health record systems have to share data this way, so the connection already exists at most care sites.

What it misses

Motor function scores and timed tests sit in clinical notes. Genetic results arrive as scanned reports, and an uncertain variant can be recorded as normal. Breathing tests come as PDFs, medication dates break, and walking status is sometimes coded for billing rather than care.

A hypothetical example to show the idea. It is not pilot data or a description of any participant. Where an element lives varies by care site and health system, so the same measure can be structured at one site and found only in notes at another.

Why this is promising

Early pilot work suggested that reading documents and notes can reach much more genetic, motor function and breathing data than structured fields alone. MDA MOVR will report the official results. Language models can now turn clinical text into standard FHIR data, and expert review keeps that work honest.

Participants authorize once, so records from every care site they've visited can follow them, past and future.

What needs engineering work

  • Extraction from documents and notes, with expert review and an audit trail
  • Mapping to standards such as SNOMED CT, LOINC, RxNorm and HGVS
  • Conflicting, missing or impossible dates
  • Telling an assessment that was done apart from its score
  • Checking output against the source documents
  • Reporting gaps honestly, so "unknown" and "not there" mean different things

Shared methods, mappings and checks are a natural fit for open source. Why contribute.
